Research

Statistical methods for combining dependent evidence, and the software and studies built on them

My research develops statistical methods for combining dependent evidence: p-value combination, signal detection and integrative association testing. The pieces of evidence to be combined, whether p-values from related tests, variants in a genomic region, or measurements from the same patients, are usually correlated. We study how to combine such evidence, how to compute the p-values of the combined tests accurately, and what the tests can detect, and we release the methods as R packages. The applications come from collaborations in genetics, neuroscience, biomechanics and clinical trials.

Topics

Combining dependent p-values

Fisher’s combination and its relatives are the workhorses of meta-analysis and of set-based association tests, but their null distributions under dependence are hard to compute. We developed p-value calculations for a general family of Fisher-type statistics under dependence (the GFisher family), truncation and weighting schemes for Fisher’s combination (TFisher), and adjustment and combination methods for discrete p-values, which arise from small-sample and exact tests. Recent work studies the correlation robustness of supremum-based p-value combination tests.

  • Gonzalo Contador and Zheyang Wu (2025). A minimum Wasserstein distance approach to Fisher’s combination of independent discrete p-values. Scandinavian Journal of Statistics, 52(3): 1281–1300. doi:10.1111/sjos.12787

Signal detection: theory and computation

When a few weak signals hide among many null features, the question is which statistics can detect them, and at what signal strength. We study detection boundaries and the finite-sample distributions of signal-detection statistics such as Higher Criticism and Berk-Jones, and we build approximations for the distributions that these and other tests need, including quadratic forms of Gaussian variables.

  • Zheyang Wu, Yiming Sun, Shiquan He, Judy Cho, Hongyu Zhao and Jiashun Jin (2014). Detection Boundary and Higher Criticism for Weak and Sparse Genetic Effects. The Annals of Applied Statistics, 8(2): 824–851. doi:10.1214/14-AOAS724
  • Hong Zhang, Jiashun Jin and Zheyang Wu (2020). Distributions and Power of Optimal Signal-Detection Statistics in Finite Case. IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing, 68: 1021–1033. link
  • Hong Zhang, Judong Shen and Zheyang Wu (2021). An efficient and accurate approximation to the distribution of quadratic forms of Gaussian variables. Journal of Computational and Graphical Statistics. doi:10.1080/10618600.2021.2000423

Integrative association testing for sequencing studies

Whole-genome sequencing studies test regions that hold many rare variants of small effect. Power depends on how the variants are weighted and how their evidence is integrated. We develop integrated weighted association tests and study the signal-to-noise ratio of genetic associations, and we apply this work in genetic studies of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) and osteoporosis. The GLOW software below implements integrative variant-set association tests with optimized weights for whole-genome sequencing studies.

  • Hong Zhang, Ming Liu, John E. Landers and Zheyang Wu (2026). Integrated Weighted Association Test with Application to Genetic Association Studies. Annals of Applied Statistics, accepted.
  • Hong Zhang, Ming Liu, Jiashun Jin and Zheyang Wu (2023). Signal-noise ratio of genetic associations and statistical power of SNP-set tests. Annals of Applied Statistics, 17(3): 2410–2431. doi:10.1214/22-AOAS1725
  • Xiaohui Chen, Hong Zhang, Ming Liu, Hong-Wen Deng and Zheyang Wu (2022). Simultaneous detection of novel genes and SNPs by adaptive p-value combination. Frontiers in Genetics. doi:10.3389/fgene.2022.1009428

Clinical and biomedical studies

With clinicians and engineers we design and analyse studies where the statistical question is part of the science: augmenting clinical trial data with external controls, and an assumption-agnostic deep learning framework for trial monitoring; predicting outcomes from biomechanical models of atherosclerotic plaques and of the brain under impact; and predicting patient-reported pain from wearable sensors.

  • Shaoming Yin, Jianchang Lin and Zheyang Wu (2026). Augmenting clinical trial data with external controls through energy balancing weighted power prior. Statistics in Biopharmaceutical Research. doi:10.1080/19466315.2025.2587049
  • Shaoming Yin, Zheyang Wu and Jianchang Lin (2026). Assumption-agnostic deep learning framework for holistic clinical trial monitoring. Therapeutic Innovation & Regulatory Science, 60: 782–797. doi:10.1007/s43441-026-00915-1
  • David Clement, Emmanuel Agu, Atifa Sarwar, Deepak Kumar, Zheyang Wu and Jean A. King (2026). Machine learning Prediction of Self-Reported Real-World Knee Osteoarthritis Pain from Wearable Accelerometer-Derived Gait Features. Scientific Reports, accepted. doi:10.1038/s41598-026-64594-6
  • Nan Lin, Shaoju Wu, Zheyang Wu and Songbai Ji (2024). Efficient Generation of Pretraining Samples for Developing a Deep Learning Brain Injury Model via Transfer Learning. Annals of Biomedical Engineering, 52(10): 2726–2740. doi:10.1007/s10439-023-03354-3
